# Analysis

## 1. Customer Base

The analysis measures customer behavior using `customer_unique_id` rather than `customer_id`, allowing repeat purchases by the same underlying customer to be identified correctly.

The customer base is predominantly made up of one-time purchasers, while a smaller proportion of customers return for additional purchases.

This indicates that customer acquisition represents a substantially larger part of the observed customer base than repeat purchasing.

## 2. Repeat Purchasing

The overall repeat customer rate provides a baseline measure of customer retention.

Monthly repeat customer rates are used to identify whether returning-customer behavior changes over time.

State-level repeat customer rates provide additional geographic context and show whether repeat purchasing varies across customer locations.

## 3. Revenue

Total payment revenue across the dataset is approximately R$16.0 million.

Revenue was analyzed across time, customer geography, product categories, and customer types.

Product-category analysis uses item prices, while customer and order-level revenue analysis uses payment values.

## 4. Revenue Concentration

Customer revenue was ranked to determine how dependent the business is on a small number of customers.

The analysis compares the revenue contribution of the top 10 and top 100 customers with total revenue.

This helps determine whether revenue is highly concentrated or distributed across a broad customer base.

## 5. Repeat Customer Revenue

Revenue from repeat customers was compared with revenue from one-time customers.

This analysis connects customer retention with financial contribution and shows whether repeat customers represent a meaningful portion of total revenue.

Average revenue per one-time customer and repeat customer was also compared.

## 6. Product Categories

Product categories were analyzed using customer counts, item revenue, and repeat customer behavior.

This identifies categories with high customer reach and categories associated with stronger repeat purchasing.

## 7. Geography

Customer distribution and revenue were analyzed by Brazilian state.

State-level analysis helps identify geographic areas with larger customer bases, higher revenue, and different repeat-purchase rates.

## 8. Delivery Performance

Delivery performance was compared against estimated delivery dates.

Late deliveries were defined as deliveries occurring after the estimated delivery date.

Review scores were compared between late and on-time deliveries.

The results describe an association between delivery performance and customer satisfaction rather than proving that late delivery directly causes lower review scores.

## 9. Customer Cohorts and Retention

Customers were grouped into cohorts based on the month of their first purchase.

Subsequent purchasing activity was tracked across Month 0 through Month 4.

The resulting retention matrix shows how many customers from each acquisition cohort returned in later months.

Retention generally declines as more time passes from the initial purchase, making the retention matrix useful for identifying differences between customer cohorts.

## 10. Business Interpretation

The analysis suggests several areas that an e-commerce business could investigate further:

- Increasing repeat purchasing among one-time customers.
- Understanding which categories are associated with repeat purchasing.
- Investigating geographic differences in customer retention.
- Monitoring delivery performance and its relationship with customer satisfaction.
- Understanding the revenue contribution of repeat customers.
- Identifying whether customer revenue is concentrated among a small group of customers.

These findings describe patterns in the Olist dataset and should be treated as areas for further investigation rather than causal conclusions.
